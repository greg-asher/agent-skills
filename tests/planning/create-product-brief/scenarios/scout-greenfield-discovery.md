# Scout Greenfield Discovery Brief

## Opportunity

Enterprise account executives spend hours combining customer records, product usage, and market data before they can identify credible whitespace in an existing account. Most account plans list products the customer does not own, but they do not explain why an opportunity is timely or who should care.

## Users and outcome

The primary user is an enterprise account executive preparing for account planning or a customer conversation. The user needs one evidence-backed opportunity hypothesis that is worth investigating, not a long list of generic cross-sell suggestions.

Sales leaders need to understand which evidence supports the hypothesis. Revenue operations owns data access and source quality.

## Smallest value loop

1. The seller opens an assigned account.
2. Scout combines Salesforce account and opportunity history, Snowflake product usage, and approved third-party firmographic data.
3. Scout presents a small set of opportunity hypotheses with supporting and conflicting evidence.
4. The seller inspects the evidence and selects a hypothesis.
5. Scout prepares a concise account brief and a note the seller can choose to save to Salesforce.

## Product principles

- Show the source and freshness of every material claim.
- Keep the seller in control of what becomes part of the customer record.
- Prefer a few supported hypotheses over exhaustive recommendations.
- Make conflicting evidence visible.

## Boundaries

Scout does not send customer messages, change opportunity stages, recommend pricing, or write to Salesforce without a seller action.

## Success

A seller can move from an assigned account to one defensible opportunity hypothesis and a usable account brief in less time than the current manual research process. The team will compare preparation time, brief usage, and seller acceptance of hypotheses during the pilot.
