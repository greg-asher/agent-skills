# Shift Handoff Discovery Notebook

## Current Position

Hospital operations leaders want to reduce information loss during nursing shift handoffs. The emerging opportunity is not general note-taking; it is helping the incoming nurse recognize unresolved changes that require action during the next shift.

## Current Phase and Learning Objective

- Phase: middle
- Objective: Compare possible product responses and define the smallest coherent value loop.

## Desired Change

Incoming nurses should begin a shift understanding what changed, what remains unresolved, and what requires attention without reconstructing the story across several systems and verbal updates.

## Actors and Situations

- Outgoing nurse prepares and delivers the handoff.
- Incoming nurse receives the handoff and assumes responsibility.
- Charge nurse intervenes in escalations and staffing changes.
- The patient is the beneficiary and bears the risk of omission.

## Current Stories and Alternatives

One reconstructed handoff involved a medication change, pending lab result, and family concern spread across the EHR, a handwritten worksheet, and verbal conversation. The pending lab result was mentioned verbally but not visible in the worksheet.

## Opportunity Map

- Recognize meaningful changes since the previous handoff.
- Preserve unresolved items across shifts.
- Reduce duplication without replacing clinical judgment.
- Make responsibility transfer explicit.

## Product Hypotheses

- A concise change-and-open-items view may be more valuable than a generated narrative summary.
- The product may need to support the handoff conversation rather than replace it.

## Dependencies and External Anchors

- EHR: source of clinical records; specific vendor and integration surface unknown.
- Hospital policy: determines required handoff content; not yet supplied.

## Assumptions and Open Questions

- Unknown whether incoming nurses trust machine-generated prioritization.
- Unknown who can close or transfer an unresolved item.
- No product promise has been selected.

## Next Conversational Opening

Compare what the incoming nurse must understand at the end of a successful handoff with the moments where the current handoff loses that understanding.
