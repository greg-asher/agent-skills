# Discovery Briefing Storyboard

Use this as a narrative pattern. Combine, omit, or expand sections to fit the subject, evidence, and audience.

1. **Title:** subject, discovery purpose, and date.
2. **The subject in one view:** what exists, for whom, and why it matters.
3. **The value path:** the workflow that best explains the product, process, or application.
4. **Business and system shape:** the smallest actor, workflow, dependency, or architectural view needed to understand that path, derived from the discovery knowledge base.
5. **What the sources establish:** corroborated claims, observed or tested behavior, and important limits.
6. **Consequential findings:** the few findings that most change the current understanding.
7. **Data and dependencies:** only the relationships that materially shape the subject.
8. **Decisions, conflicts, and current state:** what is settled, proposed, working, partial, contradicted, or missing.
9. **Ownership and operating context:** who uses, owns, decides, and operates when known.
10. **Important unknowns:** what could materially change the current model and where the knowledge base records the gap.

For a compact subject, combine related sections. For a complex application, keep the executive story concise and move subsystem detail to an appendix or separate onboarding presentation. Do not show architecture or build-state slides for a document corpus unless the evidence supports them.
